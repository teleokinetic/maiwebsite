About

I trained as an actor at Florida State and spent my spare time working on film-school sets. Then I spent years teaching movement, a job that is mostly about noticing small things.

In August 2026 I started weekly calls with my friend Elwyn to learn how to make good videos. I had never opened DaVinci Resolve. I learned editing, graphics and motion from zero, delivered my first paid edit in October, and my first long-form project in November.

Taste, for me, grows out of appreciation: curiosity and interest that make the invisible visible, through distinctions and felt resonance. It isn't inherited; it's cultivated through engagement. My habit is simple: notice when I like something, then make it explicit. Why does this cut land? Why does that graphic feel right?

    How one stretch of the Question 9 reel was made

    November 2026

    Draft from your notes and the reel's records. Rewrite it in your voice.

    The stretch

    Nate tells the story of Daniel Kokotajlo, who left OpenAI and refused to sign a non-disparagement agreement that would have cost him about $2 million in equity. In the camera file the story takes 26 seconds. In the reel it takes 20.

    Cuts

    First I took out the pauses, the ums and a restart. Twenty-six seconds became 16.9 seconds of Nate, with a three-second gap held open at the end for a clip I didn't have yet.

    Markup

    Before making anything, I marked the cut beat by beat: where a new person comes in, where a number needs to land, where Nate should carry it alone. Two of the five beats ended up with nothing on screen, on purpose.

    Research

    Every graphic needed a source. Kokotajlo's own footage came from The Diary Of A CEO's official upload, after the first copy I found was too poor in picture and sound. The $2 million comes from his TIME100 profile. When Elwyn questioned the host's line, I checked the exact words against the captions and the waveform.

    Graphics, motion and sound

    Then the layers went on: TIME's page full screen with two highlight strokes, his name beside his head, punch-ins that move closer as Nate gets to "go away", and small sounds that land with each move: a flick on each new face, a click as the page arrives, a brush under each highlight.
