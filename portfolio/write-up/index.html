Anatomy of a Reel

20 seconds of the Question 9 reel, layer by layer. Tanner Holman, November 2026.

The stretch

Nate tells the story of Daniel Kokotajlo, who left OpenAI and refused to sign a non-disparagement agreement that would have cost him about $2 million in equity. In the raw footage, Nate takes 26 seconds to tell the story. In the reel, it takes 20.

Cuts

We start with what’s obvious: pauses, ums, and bloopers. 26 seconds of raw footage was cut down to 16.9 seconds of Nate on screen, with a 3-second gap for a clip I didn’t have yet.

Markup

Before making anything, I mark the cut beat by beat. Where does momentum lag? What would make this more visually interesting? What is the best way to make this argument visually digestible for the shorts format? Two of the five beats ended up with nothing on screen, on purpose.

Research

Graphics start from an idea. We need something here. Sometimes I know what’s available, like Daniel’s interview on The Diary Of A CEO. Other times, this means sifting through 5 to 10 interviews to find a suitable clip. Reputable news sources make for good visual support. But this also means sifting through up to 20 articles before you find one that does the job you need it to do. Then it’s about grabbing the clip, editing the clip, splicing it in. Testing it in context: is it too long? Does it add energy? Does it take it away?

Graphics, motion and sound

After we’ve found some possible sources, graphics need to be reworked for the reel format: TIME’s page full screen with two highlight strokes, his name beside his head, punch-ins that move closer as Nate gets to “go away”, and small sounds that land with each move: a flick on each new face, a click as the page arrives, a brush under each highlight. There’s a testing and sampling phase too: playing a note on the piano to see if it’s the right one, listening over and over until something fits.
